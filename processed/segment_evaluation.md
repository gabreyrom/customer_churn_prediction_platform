# RFM lifecycle segments: counts and later observed dormancy

Generated by `segments.py` from `processed/invoices.csv.gz`. Do not edit by hand. Offline only: the website shows segments but no later outcomes.

## Method

- Segment definitions are in the `segments.py` docstring and the README. Dormancy threshold 90 days.
- **Cutoffs** (medians of 9,626 reference rows: active customers first observed at least 90 days earlier, pooled over 5 weekly dates, 02 Dec – 30 Dec 2010): recency ≤ 29 days = high R; frequency ≥ 5 invoices = high F; monetary ≥ £1,522.27 = high M (last 365 days). Outcomes were not used to set them.
- **Scoring dates:** 31 Mar 2011, 30 Jun 2011, 08 Sep 2011; all later than the reference dates. Segments use only purchases on or before the scoring date.
- **Outcome:** the customer first reaches 90 days without a valid purchase within 90 days after the scoring date (label `y_90` from `build_snapshots.py`). A later return does not undo it.
- **Follow-up:** every scoring date + 90 days is on or before 08 Dec 2011, the last complete day, so every active customer has a fully observed outcome and none is excluded. Later scoring dates would have incomplete follow-up and are left out of the outcome table (they appear in the count table only).
- The three outcome windows do not overlap, but the same customer can appear on more than one date. These dates fall in periods already inspected in the model evaluation; the rates are descriptive.

### Recently acquired flag (separate from the segment)

Flagged = first observed purchase less than 30 days before the scoring date, with at least 270 days of data before it. A flagged customer is also counted in their R/F/M segment above. The first observed purchase may not be the customer's true first purchase (the data start on 1 Dec 2009).

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Recently acquired | 172 | 61.6% | 108 | 57.4% | 102 | 49.0% | 382 | 57.1% |
| Not flagged | 1,601 | 54.3% | 1,858 | 56.0% | 1,785 | 48.5% | 5,244 | 52.9% |

These are observed frequencies for groups, not predicted probabilities for individuals, and they say nothing about what contacting a customer would change. Low-R segments are closer to the threshold by construction, so their higher rates largely restate recency. The flag's rate is likewise descriptive: many first-time buyers in this data are one-off buyers.

## Limitations

- **Reference period.** All reference dates are in December 2010, the only training-period dates with a complete 365-day window. Recency in particular is seasonal (the retailer is quiet in late December), so the share of High-R customers moves with the time of year.
- **Unequal history.** A customer first seen less than 365 days ago is compared on less than a year of invoices and is more likely to be Low F / Low M; this includes every flagged customer. The reference customers themselves have between 90 and about 390 days of observed history, because the data start only a year before the reference dates. Tenure is left-truncated at 1 Dec 2009: a first observed purchase may belong to a returning customer, including some flagged Recently acquired.
- **Window shift is reduced, not removed.** Trailing-window medians still move with season and with the customer mix (see the last observation date above).
- **Small segments.** Mixed combinations (for example frequent but low spend) hold few customers; their medians and rates are unstable. Frequency and spend are strongly correlated, so the eight groups are uneven.
- **Carried over:** purchases cancelled on a later day still count; amounts are gross.
