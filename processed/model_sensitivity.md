# Model sensitivity: how predicted dormancy risk responds to its inputs (Nov 26)

Generated by `sensitivity.py` from `data/weekly_snapshots.csv.gz`. Do not edit by hand.

> **Model sensitivity, not intervention effect.** Each number is what the model's equation returns for a different set of input values. It does not say what would happen to a customer if their behaviour were changed or if they were contacted, and the model is **not validated**: its test period had already been inspected and no later independent holdout exists (`model_evaluation.md`, Section 7).

## 1. Model

- **Model:** `rfm+platt` from `evaluate_models.py`: a logistic regression on recency, tenure, frequency and monetary value (log1p for the last three, standardised with training means), then a Platt recalibration.
- **Dormancy threshold:** 90 days without a valid purchase. **Forecast horizons:** 30, 60 and 90 days (90 is exploratory).
- **Inputs** are the snapshot features of `build_snapshots.py` on a scoring date: cumulative since the first observed purchase. They are not the trailing 365-day frequency and spend used for the RFM segments.
- **Fitted on** scoring dates whose outcome window ends by 31 Dec 2010; **recalibrated on** eligible rows with windows ending by 30 Jun 2011. Outcomes are observed through 08 Dec 2011.
- **Eligibility:** at horizon h a customer needs recency ≥ 90 − h to be able to reach 90 days in time. Below that the risk is exactly 0 by arithmetic, not by estimation.
- **Reproducible:** `processed/sensitivity_model.json` holds every number needed. This script asserts that it reproduces the evaluation pipeline's `rfm+platt` prediction on all 195,217 snapshot rows.

## 2. Recency

Typical profile = the median tenure, frequency and spend of the eligible training rows at that horizon. Recency moves alone, which is realistic: it is what happens to a customer who does not buy as days pass (tenure also grows by the same days; that is included).

A risk of 0.000 marks the eligibility boundary: recency 59 at the 30-day horizon and 29 at the 60-day horizon cannot reach 90 days in time; one more day of inactivity makes the customer eligible and the risk jumps. The jump is a property of the outcome definition.

## 3. One input at a time

From the typical profile, one input moves from the 25th to the 75th percentile of the eligible training rows. Change in predicted risk, in percentage points. Frequency starts at 2 because a single invoice is not possible when the first and last purchase days differ.

| Horizon | Typical risk | Recency p25 → p75 | Tenure p25 → p75 | Frequency 2 (or p25) → p75 | Monetary p25 → p75 |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 30 days | 0.929 | 66 → 81: **+10.9** | 74 → 190: **+0.2** | 2 → 3: **-0.5** | £254 → £990: **-0.8** |
| 60 days | 0.849 | 41 → 70: **+21.8** | 60 → 182: **+3.2** | 2 → 3: **-3.2** | £285 → £1,182: **-4.0** |
| 90 days | 0.804 | 11 → 53: **+19.0** | 51 → 176: **+0.6** | 2 → 4: **-10.4** | £320 → £1,542: **-9.4** |

- Longer inactivity raises the predicted risk; more invoices and higher spend lower it. Tenure has little weight once the others are known.
- **These are not independent levers.** In real customers frequency, spend and tenure move together; a one-at-a-time change describes the equation, not a customer who could exist with only that difference.
- **No causal reading.** A lower predicted risk for a customer with more invoices does not mean that getting a customer to place one more order lowers their risk by that amount.

## 4. Limitations

- **Not validated.** The test period was inspected on Nov 12 and Nov 19 and six candidates were compared on it; `rfm+platt` had the lowest eligible-group test Brier score after the fact. No later holdout exists.
- **Cumulative inputs drift.** Tenure, frequency and spend grow with the amount of history observed, so the same values mean something different later on. The calibrator absorbed part of this for early 2011 only.
- **Level is off in the test period** (table in Section 1: mean predicted vs observed).
- **Outside the training range** (below the 1st or above the 99th percentile of eligible training rows) the equation extrapolates. The website page flags such inputs.
- One retailer, one dataset, a 90-day threshold only. The model must not be applied to another dataset without retraining or a transfer validation.
