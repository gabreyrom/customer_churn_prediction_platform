# Alert timing: 60, 30 and 7 days before an observed dormancy event (Nov 26)

Generated by `alert_timing.py` from `data/weekly_snapshots.csv.gz`. Do not edit by hand. **Offline, exploratory analysis** of the Online Retail II data. No customer was alerted or contacted; nothing here measures the effect of an alert.

> **Retrospective by construction.** The event date is known only afterwards. On the alert date nobody knew which customers were 60, 30 or 7 days from dormancy. Section 3 gives the forward-looking counterpart: among customers who looked the same on that date, how many went on to become dormant.

## 1. Method

- **Event:** a customer's recency first reaches 90 days (the existing definition; `crossing_date` in `build_snapshots.py`), on or before 08 Dec 2011, the last complete day. A later return does not undo it.
- **Scoring cadence:** the existing weekly scoring dates (Thursdays). **Lead match:** for a requested lead L, the alert date is the latest scoring date at least L days before the event, so the **actual lead is L to L + 6 days**.
- **No future information in a score.** The row used is the customer's ordinary snapshot on the alert date; features, rule status and model scores use purchases on or before that date. Weekly top-N lists are ranked among **all** customers active on that date, not among customers known to have an event.
- **Recency is fixed by the lead:** recency on the alert date = 90 − actual lead. So at a given lead every event customer has (almost) the same recency, and any recency-only alert treats them alike.
- **Period:** alert dates after 31 Dec 2010. The models were fitted on earlier dates only (train-only coefficients from `evaluate_models.fit_candidates`, no recalibration). Model horizons: lead 60 → 90-day model, lead 30 → 60-day model, lead 7 → 30-day model.
- **Repeated customers:** each event is counted once per lead. A customer with several events contributes several. The same event appears under all three leads (on three different alert dates).
- **Overlapping windows:** within one lead, an event has exactly one alert date, and in Section 3 a customer's purchase spell falls in a 7-day recency band on exactly one weekly date, so no row is repeated within a table. Rows are still not independent: the same customers recur across leads and spells, and no intervals are reported.
- **Incomplete follow-up:** an event after 08 Dec 2011 is not observed and cannot be aligned. In Section 3 a row is used only when its pending dormancy day is on or before that date, whether or not the customer returned; the rest are excluded and counted.
- **Exploratory.** These dates overlap the validation and test periods inspected on Nov 12 and Nov 19. No later independent holdout exists in this dataset.

### Were event customers on that week's top-N list?

For each event, the weekly list is built on its alert date from all active customers, and the event counts as captured if its customer is in the top N. **Capture = captured events ÷ matched events at that lead.** Ties at the cut-off share the remaining places. `random` = N ÷ active customers that week.

| Lead | Events | Ranker | Capture @50 | Capture @100 | Capture @200 |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 60 days | 3,051 | random | 2.6% | 5.2% | 10.3% |
| 60 days | 3,051 | website rule (recency, then spend) | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| 60 days | 3,051 | rfm model | 0.1% | 0.3% | 3.0% |
| 30 days | 3,933 | random | 2.5% | 4.9% | 9.9% |
| 30 days | 3,933 | website rule (recency, then spend) | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.6% |
| 30 days | 3,933 | rfm model | 0.8% | 4.6% | 15.5% |
| 7 days | 4,415 | random | 2.5% | 4.9% | 9.9% |
| 7 days | 4,415 | website rule (recency, then spend) | 1.7% | 24.9% | 72.3% |
| 7 days | 4,415 | rfm model | 15.7% | 32.5% | 64.2% |

- The website rule ranks by recency, so it reaches events only shortly before they happen. Seven days ahead, event customers have 77–83 days of recency; the list fills first with customers at 84–89 days, which is why small lists capture little. A recency-only model ranks exactly like the rule and is not repeated.
- The `rfm` model also uses tenure, frequency and spend. It captures more than the rule on short lists 7 days ahead and at 30 days, but **60 days ahead both rankers capture fewer events than a random list**: their lists are still filled with customers who are much closer to the threshold.
- A list of the N highest risks is a different target from “who is L days from dormancy”. Low capture at a long lead does not mean the list is wrong; it means the list is about nearer events.

## 3. Forward-looking counterpart: customers in the same recency band

On each alert date, every active customer whose recency says they *would* become dormant in L to L + 6 days if they do not buy again. This population is known on the alert date. **Denominator: customer-dates in the band with complete follow-up.** “Became dormant” = no purchase before the pending day.

- **Calibration:** compare each model's mean risk with the “became dormant” share in the same row. In a band the model's event (dormant within the horizon) and “became dormant on the pending day” are the same event. Complete follow-up here is slightly wider than the label rule in `build_snapshots.py`.
- **Ranking within a band:** recency is nearly constant, so recency cannot rank customers here. Lower spend alone separates future-dormant customers about as well as the rfm model (0.5 would mean no information). The website's tie-break is the opposite order, higher spend first: it favours customer value, not likelihood of dormancy.
- **Reading the base rates:** even 60 days ahead, a large share of customers in the band go on to become dormant, and the share rises as the lead shortens. An alert at a longer lead reaches more customers who would have returned anyway.

## 4. What this does and does not show

- Recency alone fixes *when* a customer can be flagged. Earlier alerts need information beyond recency, and the only such model here (`rfm`) relies on cumulative features that drift over time and is miscalibrated in later periods (`model_evaluation.md`, Section 6).
- **Not shown:** whether alerting or contacting a customer changes anything. Capture and base rates describe who became dormant, not who could have been kept. That needs a randomized or otherwise causal design.
- **Still needed for model-based alerts on the website:** window-stable features in place of cumulative ones, a model frozen before a genuinely later (prospective) evaluation, calibration checked in that later period, and a decision on the alert threshold or list size. Until then the website keeps the rule-based watchlist.
- Carried over: one dataset, one retailer, a 90-day threshold only, seasonality (one year of training), later-day cancellations counted as purchases.
