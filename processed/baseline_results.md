**Chosen: train to 2010-12-31, validation to 2011-06-30, test to 2011-12-08.** It is the latest train cutoff that keeps at least 13 validation and 10 test scoring dates at h = 90 (the previous split had 4 and 6). Trade-off: the training set is smaller (31–39 scoring dates, Mar–Nov 2010), and the validation period covers Jan–Jun 2011, when dormancy is more common (the post-Christmas lull: event rates 23% / 45% / 64% vs 15% / 34% / 53% in train). A model selected on validation is thus selected under a shifted base rate. Test (Jul–Nov 2011) has a base rate close to train. Rows whose outcome window straddles a cutoff are unused.

## 2. Models

- **constant**: the train event rate for every row (AUC undefined).
- **recency**: logistic regression on `days_since_last`.
- **rfm**: logistic regression on `days_since_last`, log1p(`tenure_days`), log1p(`frequency`), log1p(`monetary`).
- Predictors come from an explicit allowlist. Standardisation means/SDs and coefficients are fit on train only. Censored labels are dropped. Selection: lower validation Brier score per horizon. Only the chosen model is scored on test.

Why the overall AUC is so much higher: AUC is the chance that a random event row scores above a random non-event row. Overall, most non-events are customers with low recency who *cannot* become dormant within h days (at h = 30, anyone under 60 days); a recency model ranks them below every eligible customer for free. Those easy pairs dominate, so the overall AUC mostly measures that eligibility rule. Inside the eligible group, the model has to separate customers who will stay silent from those who will buy again, which is the real prediction problem, and the AUC falls accordingly. Brier is affected the same way: the many near-zero predictions for ineligible rows are almost free.

Findings within the eligible group:

- h = 30: the selected recency model ranks modestly (AUC 0.69 validation, 0.67 test) and its Brier score is **worse** than the eligible-group constant on both validation and test. Its overall Brier advantage comes almost entirely from the ineligible rows.
- h = 60: the recency model is slightly better than the eligible-group constant on Brier (validation and test) and ranks moderately (AUC 0.75 / 0.71).
- h = 90: every active customer is eligible (recency < 90 always satisfies ≥ 0), so this equals the overall result.
- rfm has a higher validation AUC than recency within the eligible group at every horizon. This is reported, not acted on; the selection stays as in Section 3 and no test number was used to choose anything.

Reading the numbers:

- The overall h = 30 AUC near 0.99 is structural, not leakage: a customer can only reach 90 days of inactivity within 30 days if their recency is already at least 60. Section 4 gives the results within that eligible group.
- On validation, rfm ranks better (higher AUC) at every horizon but has the worse Brier score, consistent with the base-rate shift between train and validation. Selection by Brier therefore favours recency; the Nov 19 calibration work should revisit this choice (for example, rfm with a recalibrated intercept).

## 5. Limitations

- Rows are weekly snapshots of the same customers with overlapping outcome windows. They are not independent, so the effective sample size is far smaller than the row count; no confidence intervals are reported yet (a customer-level bootstrap is the planned fix).
- The 90-day horizon is **exploratory**: 10 test scoring dates (Jul–Sep 2011) whose windows overlap heavily.
- Base rates shift between periods (seasonality), so the Brier score mixes ranking quality with calibration-in-the-large. Calibration is not yet assessed.
- Tenure is left-truncated at 1 Dec 2009. Later-day cancellations are still counted as purchases (cleaning rule).
- The models are not used by the website, which still shows the rule-based watchlist.
